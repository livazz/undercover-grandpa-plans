Player archetypes

Undercover Grandpa · plan updated November 2, 2026 · four player types decided, unarmed predator being written

Robots that play like players

Four player types that see only the screen, judge what is doable and make mistakes to an adjustable degree. When they fail, they go back to the passage that blocks them with another idea. For each mission, you get the passages that resist and everything that was tried.

A question about the predator

A knocked-out guard gets back up after 2 minutes of game time Read in the code (KnockoutSeconds = 120 s). Since the guards are spread over several floors, having them all on the ground at once may be impossible. I don't change the game. The robot hunts all those it can, then finishes the mission, and the table gives two figures: how many guards it knocked out in total, and how many were down at the same time. If the figure disappoints you, it's up to you to decide whether the duration should change.

1. The four player types

Each one tries to win the mission. They differ in what they allow themselves and in how they react to failure.

Unarmed predator In progress

What they look for
Knock out all the guards, then finish the mission.
Their tools
Knocking out from behind, throwing coins, hiding bodies. No tool or weapon. Keys and mission objects are still allowed.
How they decide
They pick the nearest guard they can reach from behind, wait for him to turn his back, strike, then hide the body if there is time.
Doable?
A path outside the cones up to a back. For two guards watching each other, they throw a coin or come back later.
When it fails
They flee to a hiding spot or a vent, wait for the alert to end, then resume.

Armed predator, the brute

What they look for
The same thing, a notch higher.
Their tools
The dart pistol only. They have to find it. They can be seen.
How they decide
They look for the pistol first. Then they fight at the alert: they shoot at whatever approaches, counting their darts and the sleep time.
Doable?
Enough darts for the guards in sight, and the mission finishable before the first one wakes up.
When it fails
With no darts, they reload or become an unarmed predator again.

The emergent

What they look for
Make the game's systems work for them.
Their tools
Disguise, sabotage, ducts, drugs, diversions, fire, locking characters in, hiding their objects to delay them.
How they decide
They start from what they lack, look for what can get the troublesome person out of the way, trigger it and watch who moves.
Doable?
For each step: do I have the means, did the action have an effect, long enough to get through?
When it fails
They change means, and note that this one doesn't work on this person.

The ghost

What they look for
Exist for no one.
Forbidden
Being seen, disguising, shooting, knocking out, any direct contact, and anything that makes people say "someone did this", like a valve found closed, except what passes for an accident.
Allowed
Theft as long as no one sees it being taken: people think it was a neighbor.
Doable?
A whole route outside the cones, margin included, before its time limit.
When it fails
If seen, their game is marked as a failure and they carry on, to measure what follows.

2. The tree: resume from the passage that blocks

Each mission is cut into a few real passages, not 14 steps. At each failure, the robot restarts from the beginning of that passage, without replaying the game, with another idea. It notes what worked and what didn't.

Declared limits To adjust with use: at most 8 branches per passage, at most 3 game minutes per branch, at most 40 branches per game. A limit reached is written in the result: "not found in 8 tries" doesn't mean "impossible".

How it resumes: by replay. The game has no full-game save, and writing one would touch all the code. The robot records each action with its moment, rebuilds the game and replays those actions up to the start of the passage. It also keeps a copy of its notebook at that moment.

Is it deterministic? The game is, and the rooms already rely on it. I check it at each resume: a fingerprint of the game (positions, times, state) is compared with the original's. If they differ, the branch is marked "false resume" and doesn't count.

Several blockages: a blocked passage may hide another. In that case, the bench can give the robot the missing object, only to see what follows. The branch is marked "helping hand" and never counts as a win.

3. A real decision process

  1. See. It receives what the screen shows.
  2. Remember. It updates its notebook.
  3. React. Danger nearby? It flees or hides.
  4. Choose. What it lacks, and the route that is best for its type.
  5. Act. Movement and the real buttons, nothing else.

The robot's brain never receives the simulation. It receives a sheet, the "player's view", and can read nothing else. Only one piece of the code is allowed to look at the simulation: the one that fills the sheet. That is where cheating is checked.

On the sheet, because the player sees itForbidden, because the player doesn't see it
People in the camera frame, on its floor: place, gaze direction, outfitThose on other floors or outside the frame

The plan, the known vents, the objective map and its hintsThe secret vents not yet found, the future

Where it is still cheating

  • The ideas are written by me. The most important one. The robot doesn't invent a combination that no one has thought of.
  • It doesn't look at the image. A guard hard to read by eye is still seen: legibility is not tested.
  • It knows the plan and the goals from the start, like someone who has played a lot.
  • It keeps exact measurements as long as errors are at zero.
  • The size of the camera frame is calculated, not read on screen. The player can zoom, the robot keeps the base setting.
